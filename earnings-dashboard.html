Plain English

What the jargon means

Terms that appear above, in the order you're likely to hit them.

YoY (year over year)
This quarter against the same quarter last year. Used instead of sequential growth because most of these businesses have a seasonal shape — beauty and energy drinks especially.
Gross margin
Revenue minus the direct cost of making the product, as a percentage of revenue. It tells you how much is left to pay for everything else. A drop of 340 basis points means 3.40 percentage points.
bps (basis points)
One hundredth of a percentage point. 100 bps = 1%. Finance uses it to avoid the ambiguity of "margin fell 3 percent" — which could mean 3 points or 3% of the old number.
Adjusted EBITDA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, with certain items removed — typically stock compensation, acquisition costs and one-offs. It is not a GAAP measure, and each company chooses its own exclusions, so never compare it across companies without reading both definitions.
GAAP vs non-GAAP / "adjusted"
GAAP is the audited, rules-based number. Non-GAAP is management's version with items they consider non-representative stripped out. Both are shown here; where they diverge sharply, that gap is usually the story.
Free cash flow (FCF)
Operating cash flow minus capital expenditure — cash actually left over after keeping the business running and paying for new capacity. A company can post record profit and negative free cash flow at the same time, and one here does.
RPO (remaining performance obligations)
Contracted revenue a company has signed but not yet delivered — the backlog. It is a promise, not cash: it converts to revenue only as the service is delivered, and Oracle's own management says most of its newest RPO won't touch revenue until fiscal 2028 or later.
IaaS / SaaS
Infrastructure as a service (renting raw compute and storage — where the AI demand sits) versus software as a service (renting finished applications). Oracle reports them separately and they are growing at wildly different rates.
IEEPA tariff refunds
Refunds of tariffs collected under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. e.l.f. received $50m of them in one quarter, which inflated that quarter's gross margin by roughly 10.5 percentage points. It is a one-off, and the underlying margin matters more.
Points of distribution / DPP
How many store shelves carry a product, and dollars per point of distribution — sales divided by that shelf count. Rising DPP on falling shelf count means the remaining shelves are working harder, which is what Celsius reported.
SKU rationalisation
Deliberately cutting slow-selling product variants. It shrinks reported sales in the short term by design, so a decline caused by it reads differently from a decline caused by lost demand.
Consensus
The average of analysts' published forecasts. It is not company guidance. A "miss" means the company came in under analysts' expectations, which is not the same as missing its own target.

Where these numbers came from

The statements here are substantially complete, but not line-complete

  • Every figure was gathered through web search result summaries. Direct access to sec.gov, the four investor-relations sites, businesswire, nasdaq.com and the financial-data aggregators was blocked by this environment's network egress policy, so the 10-Q and 8-K exhibits could not be read line by line.
  • Cells marked not retrieved are lines I could not confirm. They are left empty deliberately — nothing on this page is estimated, interpolated or filled in to look complete.
  • Cells marked with a † are arithmetic I performed from two confirmed figures (for example cost of revenue as revenue minus gross profit). They are labelled so you can tell them from as-reported lines.
  • Several figures are as-reported roundings — AMD reports "$11.5 billion", not a to-the-dollar revenue line — so totals in the AMD income statement will not foot exactly.
  • Verify against the filing before you trade, model or report on any of this. Treat this page as a briefing that points you at the right quarter and the right question, not as a source of record.
